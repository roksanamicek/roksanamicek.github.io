The Subway: The Heartbeat of New York

My mom is always worried for me whenever I take the train. She always thinks of the worst possible outcomes and constantly checks my location on Life360 to make sure I’m okay. The subway takes up a huge part of my day going to and from Queens and Manhattan, taking around an hour one way. At moments, I blast my playlist on Spotify with my noise-cancelling AirPods and observe the people around me. Some are half asleep, with their heads tilted downwards, while others are wide awake, having a full blown conversation on the phone at blast volume. All these people have such different things going on in their lives, yet when we’re all on the train, there is an invisible string connecting us all together.

Everyone has faced scary experiences in the subway. I myself did too. But these experiences are what remind us of what living in New York really feels like. Fear is something every New Yorker has felt at some point–the paranoia, the adrenaline rush, the silence at night. Yet that's also what keeps us going. As unpredictable as New York really is, it teaches us the value of looking out for each other and even in moments of fear, you realize that everyone on the train is experiencing the same thing which strangely makes the whole situation somewhat more comforting.

I remember one day I was on the train when a homeless man walked in. The horrible smell spread immediately. He started to nag everyone for money. When most ignored him, he became more and more frustrated, yelling and cursing, and even getting close to some passengers. People started to hold their bags tighter and some even glanced nervously at each other. But even in such moments, strangers find ways to comfort each other whether it's giving space or quietly calming someone down. During this tense long stop over the bridge, the situation finally settled and the homeless person left the train. It hit me that even in such situations, we don't panic; we observe, we become aware, and we sometimes help each other out. It's a balance of independence and comfort by others that keeps the city moving.

The subway is more than just a form of transportation; it is the heartbeat of New York City. It's what keeps it alive. Every day, millions of people step onto the stations, having a destination to go to, each carrying their own story. Every morning, there are students sitting on the seats finishing up their homework, employees eating the same container of breakfast and parents trying to soothe a crying baby in a stroller. Despite all of this noise and chaos, these moments reveal something important about the city: even in such random times like taking the train, there is a shared experience. Everyone is moving in the same direction, facing delays, crowded cars, and the unpredictability of the train, yet together, we create the energy that makes New York more alive.

Every day when I take the train to school, I see all different types of people–whether it's how they dress, act, or look. Some are scribbling notes or scrolling through their phones; others are dust-covered from long shifts, and some wear scrubs with a coffee in their hand, ready for the day ahead. Everyone is carrying their own story, yet during this brief train ride, all these stories collide. I've seen people giving up their seat for others, helping a lost passenger with directions, or having a brief conversation. These small acts show that even in such a big city, New Yorkers notice each other, look out for one another, and create a sense of comfort.

The subway is more than just getting from point A to point B; there's a stop in between for noticing the city and observing the ones around you. Each ride is filled with different small stories, but these stops aren't just pauses, they are glimpses into the city as a whole and the people who give it life.

I've always wondered how different the city would’ve been if it weren't for the subway. The subway is what connects everyone from all different boroughs even if it's in silence. Every New Yorker has such different but similar experiences that it creates a sense of understanding. Whether it's through a train delay, the overcrowding of a train car, or the small acts of kindness like giving up your seat for someone; these moments remind us that we are all a part that creates the city as a whole. Without the subway, these connections would be lost, and the city wouldn’t feel as alive or surreal.
